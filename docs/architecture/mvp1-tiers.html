Lotmark · scope and cost decision

Three roads to a live system

The domain core is built and it is the hard part. What is missing is the ability to operate it. Three go-live bars, what each buys, and what each honestly costs.

21 August 2026 Basis · verified code state, architecture document, estimate workbook 223 tests passing · 43 tables · 19 routes

01 Where we actually are

Two sentences, no inflation.

The domain core is real. Seven state machines, 29 permissions, six segregation-of-duties rules, the ISO Guide 35 statistics engine golden-tested at exact float equality, Ed25519 signatures bound to length-prefixed canonical material, an HMAC-chained append-only ledger, and row-level security forced on all 43 tables. Against 21 CFR 11, clause 11.10(g) authority checks and clause 11.70 signature–record binding are the strongest things in the build, and they are genuinely done.

What is absent is the ability to operate the system. Of 19 routes, the only POSTs are the four auth routes plus five signing acts. There is no POST /projects, no POST /studies, no way to enter a measurement. Every project, study and raw result in the system originates in the seed file. There is no PDF pipeline, so nothing the product exists to produce can leave the building. There are no background jobs, so the ledger is never anchored.

05 The T1 blockers, in build order

Seventeen blockers. The order is dependency-derived, not priority-derived — which is why the cheapest items come first and the most visible one comes late.

07 Recommendation

Aim at T1, and put it in front of the client as a priced, self-contained deliverable. 528–652 person-days, ₹0.80–0.99 Cr at mid-tier rates, 3.6–4.4 months at the workbook's own staffing ramp. It is the smallest thing that is honestly demonstrable on real materials, and it produces the artefact that wins the next conversation.

Then offer T2 as a priced option, not a commitment — contingent on a named design partner, because certificates entering someone else's GMP files is a decision about liability before it is a decision about software. And hold T3 as a clearly separable programme that can be dropped whole without invalidating a single certificate T2 issued.

What the decision actually hinges on Not the feature list — the three tiers share most of it. It hinges on who the certificate is for. A certificate nobody outside the building relies on is a T1 problem. A certificate in a customer's GMP file is a T2 problem and carries validation with it. A certificate issued by a government pharmacopoeial authority is a T3 problem and carries certification, audit and residency with it. Everything else follows from that one answer.
